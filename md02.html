📺 Replay 2 — the speech marks

WHAT YOU WROTE
''What will İ do NOW!?'' panicked Emre.
VAR DECISION
"What will I do now?" Emre gasped.
Proper inverted commas, "like this". The letter I is always a plain capital I. One ? or one ! — not both.

📺 Replay 3 — the comma that should be a full stop

WHAT YOU WROTE
The door was locked ,he couldn't get out.
VAR DECISION
The door was locked. He couldn't get out.
Two full sentences can't be joined by just a comma. Full stop — or a joining word: "…locked, so he couldn't get out."

📺 Replay 4 — adjectives in the wrong seat

WHAT YOU WROTE

VAR DECISION
It was an old, rusty flashlight.
Adjectives go before the noun, with a comma between: "a skinny, dirty man".

The Football That Remembered

From the notebook of Arthur Bailey · Hillfields, Coventry

Saturday 21st September 1940

Best match of the year today and it didn't even finish. Me, Ronnie and Billy Fenn against the Thompson twins on Vine Street, coats for goalposts, and we were winning 4–2. I'd scored three. Ronnie says two of them were his but Ronnie is a liar.

Then the siren went.

Everybody on the street stopped. Even the dog. The sound was muffled at first, like it was coming from under a blanket, and then it got loud enough to rattle your teeth. "Not again," said Billy. "That's the third time this week."

"Leave the ball!" Mum shouted from the front door. "Arthur! Leave it and get in the shelter!"

I did not leave the ball. I have had that ball since I was seven. I ran back for it while Ronnie was yelling at me, and I picked it up and I ran frantically down the entry to our garden and into the Anderson shelter with Mum and our Peggy and the Fenns from next door.

Inside it was dark and it smelled of wet earth. We could hear the planes. They sound different from ours — a sort of growl that goes up and down.

"Did you get your ball, then?" whispered Peggy.

"Course I did," I whispered back.

When we came out, the street was deserted and the coats were still there for goalposts. Nobody came back to finish the match. But I've still got the ball, and tomorrow I'm going to finish it.

Arthur

The four speech rules

  1. Inverted commas go around only the words spoken: "Not again," said Billy.
  2. Speech starts with a capital letter: Mum shouted, "Leave it!"
  3. Before the closing inverted comma there is always punctuation: "Course I did," I whispered.
  4. New speaker, new line.

Part 4 · 3 min

Three new words

muffled
adjective

A sound that is quiet and not clear, as if something is covering it.

He heard muffled voices through the wall.

frantically
adverb

In a wild, hurried, panicky way.

She searched frantically for her keys.

deserted
adjective

Empty of people; everyone has left.

At midnight the stadium was deserted.
