The Football That Remembered

Chapter 2 · Vine Street

The shaking stopped as suddenly as it had started. So did the siren. Emre opened his eyes.

He was not in the attic.

He was standing in the middle of a narrow street, in the last grey light of an evening, holding the leather ball against his chest. Rows of small brick houses leaned towards each other on both sides. There were no cars. There were no streetlights. Two coats lay folded on the cobbles a few metres apart, and beyond them, two more. Goalposts.

It was the most eerie silence Emre had ever heard. No traffic. No hum of fridges or televisions. Just a dog barking somewhere far away, and the sound of his own breathing.

"Oi! You're in my goal."

A boy was standing on the pavement. He was about Emre's age, maybe smaller, with a grey jumper too short at the wrists and hair that stuck up at the back. His knees were filthy. He was looking at the ball in Emre's arms as if it belonged to him. Which, Emre realised, it did.

"You from London?" said the boy. "You look like a London kid. You an evacuee?"

"I'm from… here," said Emre. His voice sounded strange in the quiet. "Coventry."

"Never seen you." The boy held out both hands. "Ball."

Emre didn't move. His fingers wouldn't let go. "Are you Arthur?" he said. "Arthur Bailey?"

The boy's eyes narrowed. "Who's asking?"

"Emre. Emre Kaya."

"That's not a name," said Arthur, but he said it with half a grin, and then the grin went and he looked up at the sky, and Emre understood that he was listening. Somewhere far off, very faint, a sound was beginning. Low. Rising.

"That's the second tonight," Arthur muttered. "They don't normally come twice." He looked at Emre properly for the first time. "My dad was in the last war," he said, as if that explained something. "The Great War. He says wars start because countries are like lads in a playground. One lot gets bigger boots, so the next lot wants bigger boots, and everyone picks a gang, and then somebody shoves somebody and the whole yard's fighting." He shrugged. "He says the rivalry starts years before the first punch."

The siren climbed. Lights went out in the windows, one after another, like eyes closing.

"Shelter," said Arthur. "Now."

He grabbed Emre's sleeve and they ran — down the street, through a gap between two houses so narrow Emre's shoulders scraped the brick, across a scrap of garden, and down three steps into a curved metal shed half buried in the earth. It smelled of wet soil and paraffin. A woman with a headscarf and a small girl with plaits were already inside, and neither of them looked surprised to see a strange boy.

"Sit," said the woman. "Heads down."

They sat. The siren was everywhere now. And under it, another sound. A growl that went up and down.

"Whatever happens," Arthur whispered, so close that Emre felt the words on his ear, "don't let go of that ball. It's the only thing that's yours here."

Emre nodded. He was not sure why. The girl with plaits reached over and, without a word, put her hand on the ball too. That was loyal, Emre thought. She didn't even know him.

Then, far away, a muffled thump. The ground shivered. Dust fell from the roof of the shelter in a soft, slow curtain.

The ball, against his chest, went cold.

Colder.

"Emre? Emre! What on earth are you doing up there?"

Babaanne's voice. The attic. Grey light through the little window, dust, cobwebs, the folded blanket, the crate with ARTHUR painted on the lid. Emre was sitting exactly where he had sat down. His heart was going like a drum.

The ball in his hands was warm now. Not attic-warm. Warm like something that had just been held by somebody else.

He looked down at the notebook, still open on the boards beside him, and his breath stopped. Under Arthur's first entry, in the same small, slanting pencil, a line had appeared that he was certain — completely certain — had not been there before.

Sat 21st Sept, later. Second raid tonight. Met a strange boy in my goal. Says his name is Emre. Says he's from here. He isn't.

Reading questions

Five to tap, then two to write in your notebook. The written ones are where the real marks are.

The 2-mark trick: Point + Evidence

On Monday you wrote: "the author is trying to make the story feel more tense." Good point — but only 1 mark. The second mark is for evidence: a word or phrase copied from the text, in inverted commas, with because.

Point: The author wants to build tension.
Evidence: …because he ends with a "siren" and the floor "began to shake", so the reader wants to know what happens next.

Q6 · In your notebook · 2 marks

How can you tell that Arthur is used to air raids? Give a point and a piece of evidence from the text.

Point: Arthur isn't frightened; he acts fast and calm. Evidence: he says "That's the second tonight" and "They don't normally come twice", and he knows exactly where to go — "Shelter. Now."
Any point + any matching quotation = 2 marks. Point with no quotation = 1 mark.
Q7 · In your notebook · 2 marks

Why do you think the author makes the ball go cold in the shelter and warm in the attic? Explain using evidence.

Point: The ball is what carries Emre between 1940 and now. Evidence: it goes "cold. Colder." just before he leaves 1940, and back in the attic it is "warm like something that had just been held by somebody else" — which suggests Arthur really was holding it.
Bonus idea: the new line in the notebook proves the trip really happened.
Part 4 · 7 min

Why the Great War started

Arthur's dad had it right. Your class calls it M.A.I.N. — here it is in football.

M · MilitarismCountries built bigger armies and bought more weapons to look strong and be ready to fight.⚽ A club buys a huge squad because its rival did.
A · AlliancesCountries promised to help their allies if attacked — so one fight pulled everyone in.⚽ Two players fall out; both benches empty onto the pitch.
I · ImperialismCountries competed to control land and empires around the world.⚽ Every club wants to own more pitches than the others.
N · NationalismPeople felt very proud of their country and believed it was better than the rest.⚽ "We are the greatest — nobody can beat us."
The spark: Austria-Hungary declared war on Serbia on 28 July 1914. Because of the alliances, within a week most of Europe had picked a side — France, Great Britain and Russia against Germany and Austria-Hungary.

Your class's four reasons countries go to war — fun, money, being the best, arguments — map onto MAIN: money ≈ Imperialism, being the best ≈ Nationalism & Militarism, arguments ≈ the spark, and Alliances turn one argument into everyone's war.
Part 5 · 3 min

Three new words

eerie
adjective

Strange and frightening in a quiet, creepy way.

An eerie silence hung over the empty street.

rivalry
noun

Competition between people, teams or countries who each want to be the best.

The rivalry between Real Madrid and Barcelona goes back a century.

loyal
adjective

Always supporting someone or something, even when it's hard.

Loyal fans stayed until the final whistle in the rain.
